                     STANDARD OF REVIEW

6.  Summary judgment is appropriate where "there is no genuine
    dispute as to any material fact and the movant is entitled to
    judgment as a matter of law." Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(a).

7.  The moving party bears the initial burden of identifying the
    absence of a genuine dispute. See Celotex Corp. v. Catrett,
    477 U.S. 317, 322-23 (1986).

8.  Once met, the burden shifts to the non-moving party to
    "designate specific facts showing that there is a genuine
    issue for trial." Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc., 477 U.S.
    242, 248 (1986).

9.  A factual dispute is "material" only if, "under the governing
    law, resolution of the dispute might affect the outcome of the
    suit." Id. at 248.

                          ARGUMENT

    I. PLAINTIFF CANNOT ESTABLISH JUSTIFIABLE RELIANCE.

10. To prevail on a fraud claim under [REDACTED STATE] law,
    Plaintiff must prove: (i) a material misrepresentation; (ii)
    scienter; (iii) intent to induce reliance; (iv) actual
    reliance; and (v) damages. See [REDACTED CASE CITATION].

11. The Facility Representation concerns the physical location of
    manufacture — a matter within Defendant's specialised
    knowledge but not within Plaintiff's independent verification
    capacity prior to delivery.

12. The economic-loss rule bars tort recovery for breach of a
    contractual representation where the plaintiff suffers only
    economic damages unaccompanied by physical harm or property
    damage. See [REDACTED STATE] Restatement (Second) of Torts
    § 525 et seq.; [REDACTED STATE CITATION].

13. Here, Plaintiff's claimed injury is wholly economic: the
    $4,200,000 purchase price. No fastener was incorporated into
    an end-product that caused physical harm. No physical property
    was damaged.

14. The economic-loss rule therefore forecloses Count I as a
    matter of law. See [REDACTED STATE] Supreme Court decision in
    [REDACTED CASE] holding that "tort recovery is unavailable
    where the duty breached is rooted entirely in the contract."

    II. THE CERTIFICATION REPRESENTATION IS NOT ACTIONABLE.

15. The Certification Representation concerns Defendant's quality
    management system, a status that is, by its nature, true at
    the moment of certification.

16. Plaintiff cites no evidence that the AS9100D certification was
    inoperative at the time the representation was made.

17. Even if Plaintiff could establish a misrepresentation, the
    doctrine of [REDACTED DOCTRINE NAME] bars recovery where the
    representation concerns a future event or condition that does
    not materialise.

    III. PLAINTIFF FAILED TO PLEAD FRAUD WITH PARTICULARITY.

18. Rule 9(b) requires that "[i]n alleging fraud or mistake, a
    party must state with particularity the circumstances
    constituting fraud or mistake."

19. Plaintiff's First Amended Complaint pleads the Facility
    Representation in conclusory terms ("approximately 23%"),
    without identifying which of the 38 shipments were
    non-conforming.

20. The Rivera Report, cited in paragraph 13 of the Complaint, is
    inadmissible hearsay absent the testimony of its preparer.

                          CONCLUSION

21. For the foregoing reasons, Defendant respectfully requests
    that the Court grant summary judgment in Defendant's favor on
    Count I and dismiss the fraud claim with prejudice, reserving
    all rights with respect to Counts II and III.
