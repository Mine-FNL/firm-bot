ORDER ON DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR SUMMARY JUDGMENT

    Before the Court is Defendant BETA CORP's Motion for Summary
Judgment on Count I (Fraud) of the First Amended Complaint. (ECF
No. 42.) Plaintiff ACME CORP filed an opposition (ECF No. 48) and
Defendant filed a reply (ECF No. 51). The Court held oral argument
on [REDACTED DATE]. For the reasons stated below, the Motion is
GRANTED IN PART and DENIED IN PART.

                          BACKGROUND

1.  Plaintiff brings this action alleging fraud and breach of
    contract in connection with a five-year Supply Agreement under
    which Defendant agreed to manufacture and sell specialised
    titanium alloy fasteners to Plaintiff.

2.  Plaintiff contends that approximately 23% of the delivered
    fasteners were manufactured at a sub-supplier facility, in
    breach of the manufacturing-location representation in Article
    4 of the Agreement.

3.  Plaintiff further contends that the heat-treatment records
    supplied with the deliveries were fabricated, in breach of
    the certification representation in Article 7.

                          LEGAL STANDARD

4.  Summary judgment is appropriate where "there is no genuine
    dispute as to any material fact and the movant is entitled
    to judgment as a matter of law." Fed. R. Civ. P. 56(a).

5.  The moving party bears the initial burden of identifying the
    absence of a genuine dispute. Celotex Corp. v. Catrett,
    477 U.S. 317, 322-23 (1986).

                          DISCUSSION

    I. The economic-loss rule does not bar Count I as a
       matter of law.

6.  Under [REDACTED STATE] law, the economic-loss rule bars tort
    recovery only where the plaintiff suffers purely economic
    damages with no accompanying physical harm or property
    damage.

7.  Plaintiff alleges that the non-conforming fasteners were
    incorporated into a sub-assembly that subsequently failed
    during acceptance testing, destroying a prototype unit
    valued at approximately $740,000.

8.  Because Plaintiff alleges physical damage to a tangible
    prototype, the economic-loss rule does not foreclose Count I
    at this stage.

    II. A genuine dispute exists as to the manufacturing
        location.

9.  Plaintiff has produced expert testimony (the Reyes Report,
    Exhibit A) that the volume and cadence of the deliveries
    exceed the rated capacity of Lines 1 and 2 at the
    [REDACTED FACILITY] plant.

10. Defendant has produced no contrary evidence on the
    capacity-of-lines issue.

11. A reasonable trier of fact could find, on the basis of the
    Reyes Report alone, that not all 38 deliveries could have
    been produced at the [REDACTED FACILITY] plant.

    III. Plaintiff's fraud claim survives Rule 9(b).

12. The First Amended Complaint identifies the specific
    representations challenged (Articles 4 and 7), the time
    period of the alleged fraud, and the quantum of damages.

13. Although Plaintiff does not identify by serial number the
    specific fasteners produced at the sub-supplier facility,
    Rule 9(b) requires only that Plaintiff "state with
    particularity the circumstances constituting fraud," Fed.
    R. Civ. P. 9(b), and the Complaint does so.

14. Whether the heat-treatment records are inadmissible hearsay
    is a question of admissibility for trial, not for summary
    judgment.

                          CONCLUSION

15. For the foregoing reasons, Defendant's Motion for Summary
    Judgment on Count I is GRANTED IN PART to the extent the
    Court dismisses Defendant's argument that the economic-loss
    rule bars Count I as a matter of law, and DENIED IN PART in
    all other respects.

16. The Court will set a status conference by separate order.
